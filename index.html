Autonomous deep-space operations are fundamentally limited by reliance on Earth-bound radiometric networks (DSN), which suffer from multi-hour light travel latency and geometric dilution of precision. Classical inertial navigation systems (INS) suffer from unbounded dead-reckoning drift. Quantum Inertial Core: Cold-Atom Interferometry & Stern-Gerlach Launching

Matter-wave Mach-Zehnder interferometry, light-pulse phase equations, and dual-sensor classical hybridization.

The core sensing element employs ultra-cold 87Rb neutral atoms captured inside a 3D Magneto-Optical Trap (MOT) and cooled down to T_atoms ≈ 2.5 μK. A coherent three-pulse π/2 - π - π/2 light-pulse sequence driven by counter-propagating Raman lasers with effective wavevector k_eff acts as matter-wave optical elements.

// Relativistic Phase Difference Formulation
ΔΦ_tot = ΔΦ_propagation + ΔΦ_separation + ΔΦ_laser
// Operational Spaceflight Phase Shift Equation
ΔΦ = k_eff · a T² + 2 k_eff · (Ω × v_l) T² - α T² + φ_vib

To resolve six-axis inertial dynamics in a compact form factor without complex multi-beam fountains, the QSNS uses a Stern-Gerlach magnetic launching technique. Pulsed magnetic field gradients accelerate the atomic cloud to v_l = 8.2 cm/s along transverse axes. Alternating effective Raman wavevector signs (k-reversal) decouples linear acceleration a from Coriolis rotation rate Ω while canceling systematic light shifts.

Vibration Hybridization: Classical strapdown accelerometers and gyroscopes continuously track high-frequency mirror vibrations during the atomic cooling/interrogation dead time (t_c). In return, the absolute atomic phase projects onto the classical sensors to continuously estimate and null classical bias drift.

Section 3

Cooperative Surveillance Bot Swarm & Heterodyne Laser Metrology

Nanometer laser ranging interferometry (LRI), early-warning hazard detection, and non-gravitational force separation.

Monolithic spacecraft remain vulnerable to unmodeled non-gravitational accelerations, attitude thruster micro-shocks, and hypervelocity micrometeoroid strikes that destroy quantum fringe contrast. The QSNS surrounds the primary vessel with a distributed formation of autonomous 6U/12U surveillance bots operating at baselines from 1 km to 200 km.

Each node is linked via a coherent heterodyne Laser Ranging Interferometer (LRI) operating at λ = 1064 nm with a thermal-shielded Fabry-Pérot reference cavity. A Triple Mirror Assembly (TMA) retro-reflector has its virtual vertex co-located at each vehicle's Center of Mass (CoM) to eliminate tilt-to-length noise.

Surveillance Bot Shielding Capabilities:
  • Micrometeoroid Warning: Detects impulse impacts (10 μg @ 15 km/s) on forward sentinels, warning the primary vessel prior to strike.
  • Thruster Shock Isolation: Offloads high-rate visual inspection and communications, keeping the main vessel in a quiet dynamical state.
  • Drag vs Gravity Separation: Differential ballistic coefficients between lightweight bots and heavy primary ship separate solar radiation drag from local gravity gradients (∇g).

Reinforcement Learning Adaptive Kalman Filter (RL-AKF)

During extreme maneuvers or plasma blackout outages, standard Kalman filters diverge due to fixed process noise matrices Q_k. The QSNS integrates a continuous-action Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) network.

// Incremental Chi-Square Robust Innovation Weighting
K̄_k = P_{k,k-1} H_k^T [H_k P_{k,k-1} H_k^T + R̄_k]⁻¹
x̂_k = x̂_{k,k-1} + W_k · K̄_k · y_k
// DDPG Action Space Reward Optimization
R_k = - || r_true,k - r̂_k ||_2
Performance Benchmark: During 10-second external reference outages, the DDPG RL-AKF maintains an average spatial error of 0.6517 m. Across extended 300-second outages, error remains bounded to 14.94 m (aided by odometry) vs. > 500 m for conventional EKF filters.
